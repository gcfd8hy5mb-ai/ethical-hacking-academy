Authorized Red-Team Field Guide

Four in-depth workshops teach engagement scoping, defensible endpoint evidence, network segmentation reasoning, and executive-level reporting. This course is built around approved lab systems and observable outcomes rather than risky live-target activity.

Safety boundary: do not test unapproved systems, interact with real employee accounts, obtain credentials, evade detection, or modify security configurations. Example addresses are documentation-only; all scenario outcomes are fictional unless you independently measure them in your own authorized lab.

WORKSHOP 1 OF 4 · Engagement operations

Scope-first red-team operations and decision gates

The first professional skill is knowing what the authorized assessment must demonstrate, what to measure, and when to stop. A hypothetical enterprise has approved a limited internal tabletop and a small isolated lab. The objective is to test coordination and monitoring—not to obtain credentials or compromise users.

Authorized environment: Lab-only actors: tester, defender observer, engagement owner, safety contact. Allowed assets: LAB-WIN01, LAB-LNX01, TRAINING-APP. Forbidden: staff identities, production networks, phishing, persistence, concealment, privilege escalation, credential access, uncontrolled scanning.

Field workflow

  1. Prepare an asset and identity inventory from the exact approved list. Record legal owner and the approved source host; do not expand the list because adjacent IPs are visible.
  2. Define three separate objectives: accuracy of asset discovery by passive inventory, defender visibility for benign command execution, and readiness to triage a synthetic identity event.
  3. Write a start/stop matrix: unknown system → stop and ask; unexpected sensitive data → stop and preserve only authorized metadata; loss of monitoring → pause; business impact → notify safety contact.
  4. Establish event identifiers and synchronization. All roles use UTC timestamps, approved lab-only account names, and a shared event label such as LAB-RT-01.
  5. Write a success condition that can be evaluated without exploitation: defender accurately identifies exercise, correlates read-only telemetry and records an approved handoff.

Read-only commands or synthetic policy

PowerShell on LAB-WIN01 (owned lab):
Get-Date -Format o
whoami
Get-CimInstance Win32_OperatingSystem | Select-Object Caption,Version
Get-NetIPConfiguration | Select-Object InterfaceAlias,IPv4Address

Linux on LAB-LNX01:
date --iso-8601=seconds
id
hostnamectl
ip -brief address

Illustrative output and evidence

LAB-RT-01 | 2026-11-15T18:00:00Z | LAB-WIN01 | baseline complete
LAB-RT-01 | 2026-11-15T18:02:00Z | LAB-LNX01 | baseline complete
Approved inventory: 2/2 hosts identified
Defender visibility: pending verification

Investigator interpretation

Inventory does not prove a host is safe, and a missing log does not prove stealth. Lack of visibility is a defender signal gap to investigate; do not test evasion or disable monitoring to demonstrate it.

Troubleshooting and limits

A lab VM without a network interface may be valid if the exercise is offline. Document actual configuration, compare against authorization, and do not bridge the VM onto another network.

Independent deliverable: Completed scope register, safety matrix, UTC event list and three non-disruptive acceptance criteria.

WORKSHOP 2 OF 4 · Purple-team validation

Compare endpoint telemetry with a synthetic defender timeline

A legitimate read-only command can create useful endpoint telemetry. The task is to compare the tester's time-stamped actions with what the defender can actually confirm, including missing sources and benign explanations.

Authorized environment: One owned Windows lab VM and one Linux VM. Read-only inventory commands only. Defender gets a fabricated alert table, not access to another person's account or company logs.

Field workflow

  1. Tester records the exact approved inventory command, timestamp, host and process ID when available.
  2. Defender checks whether the sample endpoint record contains actor, host, timestamp, parent process, executable path and command line. Mark absent fields as unknown.
  3. Check time-zone conversion and clock drift before declaring unmatched activity.
  4. Classify outcomes as matched, unmatched because of missing data, or unmatched with unexplained discrepancy. Do not infer malicious activity from a mismatch alone.
  5. Write a remediation proposal: improve benign context enrichment, retention or owner-approved logging coverage without weakening protective controls.

Read-only commands or synthetic policy

Windows:
Get-Date -Format o
Get-Process | Select-Object -First 8 ProcessName,Id,StartTime
Get-Service | Select-Object -First 8 Name,Status

Linux:
date --iso-8601=seconds
ps -eo pid,user,comm --sort=pid | head
systemctl --failed --no-pager

Illustrative output and evidence

EXERCISE LAB-RT-02 (fictional)
18:03:00Z tester Get-Service on LAB-WIN01
18:03:01Z defender sees powershell.exe on LAB-WIN01
18:03:02Z defender command line missing
18:05:00Z Linux failed-unit query recorded by tester
18:05:xxZ defender Linux telemetry not provided

Investigator interpretation

The Windows host match supports a correlation but not exact command attribution if command-line capture is missing. Absence of Linux telemetry in this packet is not proof the test was undetected or monitoring absent; the dataset may simply be incomplete.

Troubleshooting and limits

Protected Windows process start times may be inaccessible; omit that field instead of elevating. On Linux systems without systemd, document the OS difference.

Independent deliverable: Tester-versus-defender correlation matrix with evidence confidence, unknown fields, and one safe improvement for each gap.

WORKSHOP 3 OF 4 · Network controls

Network segmentation evidence and controlled validation

Defenders and authorized testers need to understand rule precedence, routing, and application boundaries. This workshop uses reserved documentation addresses and a fictional first-match firewall to evaluate a narrowly scoped observation.

Authorized environment: Source LAB-WIN01 at 192.0.2.20; destination DB-SIM at 198.51.100.10; a separate approved update service. These IPs are examples only and must not be scanned or contacted. No live remote tests are requested.

Field workflow

  1. Read each fictional rule in order and mark which rule would match the explicitly listed source, destination and TCP port.
  2. Distinguish the policy decision from network path, host firewall, transport and application authentication. Each can independently block access.
  3. For a disposable private lab, design—but do not perform without specific owner approval—one bounded TCP handshake check between exact approved endpoints.
  4. Plan how a defender observer would confirm which rule handled the event and tie it to the approved exercise ID.
  5. Propose a narrow policy improvement and acceptance test that keeps the update service working while denying unintended database access.

Read-only commands or synthetic policy

Fictional FIRST-MATCH firewall:
10 ALLOW TEST_VLAN -> ANY tcp/443
20 DENY  TEST_VLAN -> DB_SIM any
30 ALLOW TEST_VLAN -> UPDATE_SERVICE tcp/443
99 DENY  ALL

Permitted local routing inspection on your own Windows VM:
Get-NetRoute -AddressFamily IPv4 | Select-Object -First 12 DestinationPrefix,NextHop

Illustrative output and evidence

Expected rule for DB-SIM tcp/443 under stated semantics: rule 10
Application authentication: NOT TESTED
Actual connectivity: NOT TESTED
Defender log verification: NOT TESTED

Investigator interpretation

Rule 10 can shadow rule 20 on the specified first-match engine. That is a policy exposure hypothesis. It does not prove a successful connection, database login or data access, and other firewall products may implement different semantics.

Troubleshooting and limits

If a future isolated test fails, do not automatically call the firewall fixed: virtual adapter mode, routing, other firewalls, listener binding and destination availability may explain the failure.

Independent deliverable: Rule-by-rule analysis, a narrowly scoped retest plan, and two separate statements for proven configuration and untested impact.

WORKSHOP 4 OF 4 · Professional capstone

Executive debrief, remediation, and safe retesting

A high-quality assessment ends with reproducible evidence, understandable risk, ownership and proof of improvement. This capstone composes the previous three workshops into a professional deliverable, without claiming any compromise.

Authorized environment: Use only the synthetic evidence in this fieldbook and your own redacted read-only lab observations. No external assessment or remote host access is necessary.

Field workflow

  1. Create the executive summary: purpose, scope, notable visibility gaps, policy interpretation, potential operational effect and boundaries of conclusions.
  2. Write a finding for the incomplete endpoint telemetry. Include evidence, alternative benign explanations, relevant owner and a measurable validation plan.
  3. Write a finding for the first-match network policy rule shadowing. Clearly distinguish actual test outcome from hypothetical reachability.
  4. Define remediation ownership, change approval, rollback trigger and defender acceptance criteria. Validate both intended blocks and allowed business traffic in a future isolated authorized test.
  5. Deliver a final debrief that separates tester intent, observed data, defender understanding, remaining unknowns and next-step verification.

Read-only commands or synthetic policy

Example evidence register (fictional):
RT-001 | LAB-WIN01 | 18:03:00Z | tester observation | read-only service query
RT-002 | LAB-WIN01 | 18:03:01Z | defender telemetry | process observed, command line missing
RT-003 | RULE-10   | documented policy | broad allow before database deny
RT-004 | DB-SIM    | NOT TESTED | no application/connection conclusion

Illustrative output and evidence

Finding A: Defender telemetry missing a command-line field in supplied sample.
Risk: reduced ability to distinguish approved from unexpected activity.
Finding B: First-match firewall policy appears to permit DB-SIM tcp/443.
Limit: no network connection or data access was attempted.
Verification: owner-approved lab retest with defender observer and rollback.

Investigator interpretation

A robust report can make an actionable recommendation without exploiting anything. Claims must be narrower than the evidence: a visible rule-order weakness is not proven exfiltration, and missing fields are not proof of stealth.

Troubleshooting and limits

When a mitigation breaks approved traffic, revert through change control and document the observation instead of bypassing logging or allowing broad access.

Independent deliverable: Executive summary, two substantiated findings, evidence register, acceptance tests, unresolved questions and signed-off debrief checklist.
